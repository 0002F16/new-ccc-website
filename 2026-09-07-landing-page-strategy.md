# Capital Career Club: landing-page and marketing strategy

7 September 2026. Internal strategy brief; draft copy is not a verified performance claim.

## Recommendation

Position CCC as **a job-search team for experienced international professionals in Poland**. Lead with the outcome the visitor wants—interviews and a role that reflects their experience—then explain the done-for-you service as the mechanism.

Use two campaign entry points into the same offer:

1. **Experienced but getting no interviews.** Strongest broad pain signal in the CRM.
2. **Employed but underpaid or stuck below your level.** Smaller observed segment, with particularly relevant experienced-professional examples. Prioritize testing this segment; the data does not establish superior profitability or conversion.

The landing page should help suitable prospects understand the service and apply for a fit call. Raw booking volume is an inadequate success measure given the qualification and affordability problems recorded in the CRM.

## Evidence and limits

Read-only production CRM snapshot taken at 2026-09-07 12:33 UTC: **1,004 lead records, of which 220 are B2C and 784 B2B**. All analysis below excludes B2B. No obvious test/demo name matches were flagged; this is not a complete deduplication audit. Counts describe records, not necessarily unique humans.

Reviewed all B2C booking-reason fields and substantive sales notes, including meeting summaries already stored in the CRM. No recordings, source sheets, external profiles, or competitors were fetched. Anonymous L identifiers below refer to the working snapshot, not public testimonials.

Current B2C stages: 151 Lost, 38 Offer / Deciding, 27 Won, 2 Call Booked, 1 Contacted, 1 Call Done. **Won is a recorded sale stage, not a job placement.** There are 33 membership-linked leads; historic community membership is not equivalent to purchasing today's DFY offer. The history contains different prices, coaching offers, locations and eligibility standards. It is not a clean conversion cohort for the current product.

For multi-select pain answers, HTML was stripped, the specific challenge-answer block isolated, and each exact option counted once per lead across repeated intake blocks. These are prompted selections, not spontaneous quotations. Free-text and sales-summary examples were reviewed separately.

| Signal | Observed evidence | Implication |
|---|---|---|
| Applications produce no interviews | 99/145 challenge respondents selected no interview invitations (68%) | Start with a recognizable, concrete problem. |
| Profile and application problems | 55/145 selected CV/LinkedIn not noticed; 47/145 ineffective application methods | Show the work performed and how targeting, positioning and execution connect. |
| Interviews fail to become offers | 43/145 selected failing interviews; 28/145 lack confidence | Include interview preparation in the process, without making it the only headline. |
| Underpaid and stuck | 34/145 selected low-paying work and difficulty switching (23%) | A credible second campaign, reinforced by detailed experienced-client notes. |
| Urgency | 143/163 with a normalized timeline selected needing a job within two months (88%) | Explain onboarding and immediate work clearly; urgency is not evidence that CCC can guarantee a deadline. |
| Qualification | 42/151 Lost records marked Unqualified (28%) | State audience and fit requirements before booking. This label mixes skill fit and affordability in some notes. |
| Price friction | 16/151 Lost marked No budget, 5/151 Too expensive (14% combined) | Explain the full fee before a call and distinguish affordability from uncertainty about value. Additional money objections occur under other stages/reasons. |
| Historical service preference | 12/76 selected fully handled; 29 self-directed with guidance; 14 somewhat busy; 21 unsure | Do not assume all historical leads wanted DFY. Teach the service model and qualify explicitly. |

Percentages have different denominators because fields are incomplete. Pain categories overlap. The normalized single challenge field says 106/146 no invites and only 4 low pay; it loses secondary multi-select answers. The raw-answer analysis is more useful for copy.

The CRM cannot establish your best acquisition channel: imports default to LinkedIn, while Calendly records booking infrastructure rather than original attribution. Nor can role level establish three years of experience, or salary parsing establish disposable income. No market-wide conclusions or placement-rate claims follow from this dataset.

## What the notes add

**Experience without recognition.** L0135 describes a good profile and relevant languages but no interviews. L0083 reports previous work at good companies followed by eight months without interviews. The useful emotional territory is frustration at not being recognized for existing ability. Their belief that ATS is the cause is a customer perception, not a proven technical diagnosis.

**Career stagnation, not simply unemployment.** L0739 describes three years without promotion, concern about restructuring, and feeling underpaid; the CRM currently marks this lead Won. L0187 wants to pivot without making previous experience feel worthless. These support copy about progressing from existing skills rather than starting over.

**Execution relief has concrete value.** L0991 describes exhausting night shifts and insufficient energy to search consistently. L0988's stored meeting summary records selecting DFY and paying the setup fee after a prolonged unsuccessful search. This supports showing how delegation changes the client's weekly workload. It does not establish that delivery produced a job.

**Affordability and trust are different objections.** L0062's notes describe interest in DFY but concern about cash flow and the combined upfront/success fee. L0005 wants clarity about the process and likelihood of results. L0791's summary describes preferring a cheaper coaching offer despite relevant experience; its current Lost/Unqualified label shows why sales notes and stages should not be treated as interchangeable.

**Some visitors misunderstand the business.** L0090's booking answer sounds like an application to CCC's engineering team. Clearly state that this is a paid service for job seekers, what it does, and who pays.

**Founder trust matters, without proving channel ROI.** Several booking reasons cite LinkedIn conversations, following the founder, or referrals. L0065's notes describe a prospect needing introductory YouTube resources before a meaningful sales discussion. Connect content to a clear explanation of the service before the call.

## Website diagnosis

The [current homepage](https://www.capitalcareerclub.com/en) includes graduates and survival-job transformations prominently and splits attention between enrollment, a strategy call, and a free community. Its broad promise and junior examples do not consistently reflect the owner’s desired experienced-professional audience.

The [checkout](https://www.capitalcareerclub.com/en/checkout) explains DFY more concretely: materials, applications, outreach, interview support and negotiation. Bring that explanation forward, after confirming the current operating scope. A visitor should understand what the team actually does before being asked to buy.

The [results page](https://www.capitalcareerclub.com/en/results) combines interview invitations, assessments and offers, including multiple milestones for some people. Curate three to five cases relevant to experienced professionals. Clearly label interviews, signed offers and job starts. Neither the results wall nor CRM sales stages validates a placement rate.

Use the existing [terms](https://www.capitalcareerclub.com/en/regulamin) to check offer consistency: service duration is conditional there, while checkout describes ongoing work without a time limit. This is an editorial consistency issue to resolve before carrying either statement into new copy.

## Copy angles to test

| Priority | Angle and sample headline | Support and execution |
|---|---|---|
| 1 | **You have the experience. Your job search should reflect it.** | Broad experienced-professional positioning. Immediately add Poland, the 3+ years criterion, and what DFY means. |
| 1 | **Applying in Poland, but still not getting interviews?** | Strongest pain-led acquisition hook. Pair it with credible process evidence and experienced cases, so it does not become another generic CV pitch. |
| 2 | **Your next role should build on your experience.** | Career progression, pay and avoiding a perceived restart. Use campaigns about stagnation, missed promotions and moving while employed. |
| 2 | **Your job search shouldn’t need a second shift.** | Execution relief for employed prospects. Show tasks the team takes on and the decisions the client retains. |
| 3 | **Getting interviews, but no offer?** | Focused interview-conversion content. Lead into a diagnosis of whether the full service is appropriate. |

Avoid framing the audience as desperate or deficient. Avoid guarantees of salary increases, arbitrary job deadlines, immigration outcomes, ATS bypasses, or employer partnerships unless the specific claim has supporting evidence. Existing sales summaries sometimes contain these promises; their presence is not verification.

## Proposed hero and page sequence

**Eyebrow:** Done-for-you job search · Poland · 3+ years of professional experience

**Headline:** You have the experience. Your job search should reflect it.

**Subhead:** A job-search team for expats and immigrants in Poland who are struggling to get interviews or ready to move beyond an underpaid role. We handle your CV, LinkedIn, applications and outreach, and support you through interviews and negotiation.

**Primary CTA:** Apply for a fit call

**Supporting line:** Tell us about your experience, target role and current search. We’ll assess whether the program fits your situation.

The service list is grounded in checkout and CRM summaries; confirm the final delivery scope before publishing. If the existing 30-minute call remains the format, describe its purpose and length consistently.

Recommended order:

1. Hero with audience, outcome, service and one primary CTA.
2. Two recognizable situations: unsuccessful applications; underpaid/stalled career.
3. Three strong experienced-client cases, each showing starting point, obstacle, work performed, and verified outcome/date.
4. What the team does and what the client still does: accurate information, target approval, interview attendance and offer decisions. Make approval/control expectations explicit.
5. How the first weeks work: assessment, positioning, approved search criteria, execution, progress review, interview preparation. Publish actual operational timings only.
6. Fit criteria: in Poland, 3+ years of relevant experience, suitable target roles, willingness to participate, and ability to fund the service. Assess work authorization/logistics without implying immigration services.
7. Full price explanation and conditions, followed by objections and FAQs.
8. Repeat the fit-call CTA. Put free educational resources in a secondary route for people outside the paid offer's scope.

FAQ priorities: Is this coaching or execution? Which roles do you support? Who approves applications? What must I do? How are progress and quality reviewed? What if I get interviews independently? What triggers the success fee? What if no suitable offer arrives? Can I search while employed? Answer from the actual agreement and operating model.

## Pricing copy needs one decision

The owner's stated offer is **6,000 PLN plus 23% of salary for the next three months once hired**. Checkout's wording can instead be read as 23% of one gross monthly salary divided across three payments. Do not leave visitors to interpret this.

Assuming the intended rule is 23% of each of the first three monthly salaries, use: **“6,000 PLN setup fee, plus 23% of your [confirmed salary basis] in each of your first three months after you’re hired.”**

Illustrative arithmetic only, before any cap or other conditions: at a salary basis of 12,000 PLN/month, that means 2,760 PLN per month for three months, plus 6,000 PLN setup: 14,280 PLN total. If instead 23% of one month is divided into three payments, the total is 8,760 PLN. This distinction materially affects the offer.

Confirm salary basis, tax-inclusive setup price, the checkout raise cap and how it applies to unemployed clients, fee trigger, service duration and available installment terms. Historical negotiated plans are not automatically today's standard offer. Do not say “we only get paid when you do,” because the setup fee contradicts it.

## Marketing execution and measurement

**LinkedIn:** Test two matched campaigns: experienced professionals receiving no interviews, and employed professionals facing stagnation. Keep the qualification criterion visible. Use case breakdowns that explain starting point, intervention and outcome; avoid a wall of employer logos without context. Compare messages within the same audience before attributing results to the headline.

**YouTube:** Build service education around CRM questions: “Good experience, no interviews: what should you review?”, “What a done-for-you job-search team actually does”, “Changing jobs while working full time”, “How the CCC fees work”, and an experienced-client case breakdown. Titles are content proposals, not market research findings. End each with the same fit-call route.

**Warm content:** Address the observed process, proof and fee questions with a service walkthrough, relevant case and complete price explanation. No outreach was sent as part of this analysis.

Test order:

1. Replace broad graduate framing with experienced-professional framing and visible fit criteria.
2. Compare no-interview and career-upgrade messages with audience/source tracked separately.
3. Test a concrete service walkthrough plus matched proof against generic testimonials.
4. Test pricing explanation and qualification placement; track whether call quality improves even if raw bookings decrease.

Measure visitor → application → qualified applicant → attended call → paid enrollment. Record acquisition source/UTM, message variant, relevant years of experience, current employment, chosen offer and specific objection. Keep client placements and time-to-offer in a separate delivery dataset with explicit denominators. Report counts alongside rates and avoid declaring winners from a handful of sales.

The central recommendation is evidence-led, but still a test: **sell recognition of existing experience and a concrete team doing the work; qualify for the premium service early.**
