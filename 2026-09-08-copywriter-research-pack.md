# Capital Career Club — copywriter research pack

**Prepared:** 8 September 2026. **Use:** internal research and messaging guidance for an English landing page. **Audience:** skilled expats and immigrants in Poland with at least three years of professional experience. Give equal weight to people struggling to find work and employed people seeking to leave underpaid or stagnant roles.

This is a companion to [the original strategy brief](2026-09-07-landing-page-strategy.md), which is preserved. It is not finished website copy. Illustrative lines below are creative hypotheses, not measured winners or verified performance promises. Private customer language is research material, not permission to publish a testimonial.

## 1. Copywriter handoff: the argument to build

**Recommended central message:** A job-search team for experienced international professionals in Poland, helping them make their next move with the experience they already have.

**Emotional thread:** “I have built real skills. Why is that not translating into interviews, progression or a suitable next role?” The opportunity is to recognize existing competence and explain concrete help. Avoid implying that every prospect is objectively underpaid or that every rejection is caused by their international background.

**Commercial argument:** The buyer has usually tried something already. Explain why paying for a team changes the work being done: clearer targeting, stronger presentation, consistent applications and outreach, and support through interviews. Show how those activities connect. A list of documents alone makes the offer look like expensive CV editing.

**Two equally important entry situations:**

- **Stalled search:** “I am making an effort and getting little useful response.” Lead with recognition, clarity about the next steps and evidence relevant to their background.
- **Stalled career:** “My current role has stopped giving me room to grow, but I still need to keep working.” Lead with progression, continuity of experience and manageable participation.

**Essential proof:** one comparable case per situation; the exact service those clients received; an accurate outcome label; and a real example of the work the team performs. Historical coaching success can support CCC's knowledge, but must not be represented as current DFY execution evidence.

**Primary CTA recommendation:** “Apply for a fit call.” Use the same action throughout. This is a proposed funnel choice, not a description of an already implemented application process. The call should assess background, target and fit, with clear expectations about a paid service.

**Do not carry into the draft:** unsupported placement percentages, guaranteed timelines, blanket ATS explanations, claims of guaranteed employer access, invented weekly service standards, or the phrase “we only get paid when you do.” The owner confirms an upfront fee.

## 3. Audience profiles: two routes, one service

| Dimension | Experienced professional with a stalled search | Employed professional with a stalled career |
|---|---|---|
| Starting situation | Repeated applications, few useful responses, sometimes a recent job loss | Low perceived pay, no progression, disappointing management conversations or concern about future stability |
| Functional progress sought | Relevant interviews and a viable next role | A move that improves role fit, growth or compensation without discarding existing experience |
| Emotional stakes | Confusion about rejection; confidence that previous achievements still matter | Professional identity, frustration at stagnation and concern about staying too long |
| Observed triggers | Months of unsuccessful applications; rejection/silence after effort; watching relevant success stories | No promotion, restructuring or offshoring, another unproductive manager conversation |
| Why DFY might appeal | An organized alternative after independent applications fail | Someone to sustain the agreed search work while the client remains employed |
| What could stop purchase | Cash-flow limits, doubts about results, waiting for an existing interview, uncertainty over fees | Cost/value doubts, scarce time for participation, reputation/control concerns, deciding to do it independently |
| Proof needed | A comparable starting profile and the actual work/results, with dates | A comparable employed client, continuity of experience, and a clear participation/approval process |
| Page emphasis | “You have tried. Here is what changes in the search.” | “Move forward from where you are. Here is how the work is shared.” |

The first five rows synthesize observed CRM situations. Client-control and reputation concerns are sensible **questions to answer for a delegated service**, not measured frequent objections in the snapshot. Both audiences may have interviews already; the distinction is starting situation, not an exclusive funnel stage.

**Shared belief to build:** “This team understands my background, can explain the work, and will help me pursue agreed roles without making claims or decisions I have not understood.” Operational control must be confirmed before the writer promises it.

**Do not collapse the profiles:** financial desperation is not the defining story for employed movers; free time is not guaranteed for unemployed people; wanting higher pay does not automatically mean affordability; relevant experience does not prove suitability for an unrelated senior role.

## 5. Positioning territories, ranked

### 1 — Recognition of existing experience

**Strategic interpretation:** The shared problem is a gap between accumulated experience and the opportunities the person is currently getting. This is broader than a CV problem and specific enough to recognize both audiences.

**Illustrative headline:** “You have the experience. Your next move should build on it.”

**Category line:** “A done-for-you job-search team for experienced international professionals in Poland.”

**Support:** L0135 cannot understand interview silence despite a good profile; L0083 cites experience and recognizable employers; L0739 describes stalled progression. The selected no-interview pain supplies breadth, while direct wording supplies emotional meaning.

**Writer's job:** acknowledge the effort already made, state the audience, then explain the service. Do not leave the page at an abstract affirmation. Move quickly to targeting, presentation and agreed search execution.

**Limit:** This does not prove that employers are undervaluing everyone, that the candidate meets every role requirement, or that CCC can guarantee recognition. Test the angle; it is not a validated conversion winner.

### 2 — Career progression without starting over

**Strategic interpretation:** “Better job” can mean preserving the value of prior years while changing role, employer, pay or direction. This is particularly relevant to the employed half of the page and can also resonate after unemployment.

**Illustrative headline:** “Change direction without leaving your experience behind.”

**Support:** L0187 explicitly describes avoiding a restart; L0739 wants progression beyond a stalled role; L0792 wants a next role as an existing position is offshored.

**Writer's job:** show transferable experience, credible target roles and a comparable case. Connect progression to the client's actual background, rather than promising any desired career pivot.

**Limit:** L0187's preferred involvement is self-directed guidance. Strong resonance does not establish a desire to pay for DFY. Whether particular career pivots qualify is a business decision.

### 3 — Practical relief from search execution

**Strategic interpretation:** The premium service becomes understandable when the buyer sees which recurring work the team takes over, and which decisions and responsibilities stay with the client.

**Illustrative headline:** “A team to keep your job search moving.”

**Employed variant:** “Keep working while a team supports your next move.”

**Support:** L0991 describes exhausting shifts; L0997 describes applications during breaks; L0988's sales summary records choosing DFY after independent effort. Checkout lists execution-related activities.

**Writer's job:** show one real week or progress update, one targeting rationale and the actual responsibility split. Explain quality before application volume. Tie each service activity to the buyer's situation.

**Limit:** No verified hours-saved figure, confidentiality policy or weekly cadence is established here. Only 12/76 historical involvement answers selected fully handled; the page must explain and qualify the premium option.

**Recommended combination:** Territory 1 organizes the page; territory 2 gives career movers equal recognition; territory 3 makes the service and price intelligible. Use separate campaign hooks but one coherent offer. Interview-conversion content can support the journey without becoming a fourth competing homepage proposition.

### Writing instructions

- Use plain English and concrete verbs: review, define, prepare, apply, contact, practice. Explain “DFY” on first use rather than assuming familiarity.
- Address effort already made. Avoid “you just need to try harder,” inspirational filler and language borrowed from dismissive rep notes.
- Use the audience's emotional meaning without publishing intimate disclosures about self-worth or finances as testimonial copy.
- Balance desired outcomes with what CCC can actually commit to delivering. “Supports your next move” is different from a guaranteed job offer.
- Do not imply that English ability or an international name explains rejection without individual evidence.
- Treat illustrative headlines as a direction for writing, not mandatory final language. The approved deliverable is a research pack.

**Arithmetic illustration, not proposed terms:** assuming 23% of each of three salaries with a 12,000 PLN monthly basis, the success component would be 2,760 × 3 = 8,280 PLN, plus 6,000 PLN setup, totaling 14,280 PLN before any cap or other conditions. If 23% of one month is split across three payments, the corresponding total is 8,760 PLN. Neither interpretation should be silently selected for publication.
